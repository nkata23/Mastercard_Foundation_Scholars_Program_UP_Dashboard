Key Insights

Data-Driven Findings from the July Scholar Dataset

704 unique scholars · 746 enrolments · 31 countries

Geographic Reach
The Mastercard Foundation Scholars Program at the University of Pretoria reaches 31 countries. South Africa leads with 188 scholars (26.7%), followed by Zimbabwe (88), Kenya (72), and Eswatini (69). These four alone account for 59.2% of all scholars, yet the programme spans every major African sub-region.
Employment Outcomes
Of 463 exited enrolments, 164 (35.4%) are confirmed employed. Education & Academia absorbs 71 graduates - 32.0% of known placements - creating a strong academic pipeline. 155 (33%) are still "to be tracked," a critical alumni data gap.
Gender Equity Shift
A striking reversal: Phase 1 was 58% male; Phase 2 is 58% female. Overall balance is 49.1% F / 50.9% M - near perfect parity across the full cohort. The Phase 2 gender shift signals intentional equity correction in scholar selection.
Programme Scale-Up
Enrolments surged in Phase 2: 134 in 2025 and 161 in 2026, vs an average of only 40/year in Phase 1. Phase 2 already accounts for 45.7% of all enrolments in just 3 years, representing a 3× scale-up in annual intake.
Academic Concentration
EBIT (31.9%) and Natural & Agricultural Sciences (28.0%) account for 59.9% of enrolments. Masters is the dominant level (37.7%). Phase 2 is diversifying: Education grew from 2 to 29 enrolments, and UG Degrees from 103 to 143.
Attrition & Risk
30 dropouts (4.0%) and 19 dismissals (2.5%) give a combined attrition rate of 6.6%. Honours shows the lowest active rate (22.6%), as most have progressed or exited. Early-year monitoring remains critical to reducing preventable attrition.
Internship Impact
Of the 168 scholars (23.9%) who completed internships, 82% (137) have already exited, compared to 58% in the non-internship group - suggesting internships accelerate career transitions. The SEF further supported 40 scholars (5.7%) through enterprise pathways.
Diversity and Inclusion
The Mastercard Foundation Scholars Program at the University of Pretoria includes 42 scholars affected by forced displacement (6.0% of cohort) - comprising 35 refugees hosted across 10 countries, 5 asylum seekers, and 2 IDPs from Cameroon. South Africa hosts the largest refugee group (16 scholars). A further 13 scholars (1.8%) have declared disabilities across 8 types, including visual impairment (3), psychiatric conditions (2), speech impediment (2), physical impediment (2), and dyslexia (1). Both groups represent high-need subgroups requiring dedicated support frameworks within the programme. See the Diversity and Inclusion section above for the full breakdown.
Data Quality Note
Alumni tracking shows 33% "to be tracked" among exited scholars. The SEF reached 40 scholars (5.7%) through social enterprise pathways. Age peak (58%) falls in the 25-34 year range - the core emerging leader cohort targeted by the programme.
